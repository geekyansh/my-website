SEO vs AEO vs GEO: What Is the Difference and Which One Do You Need?

By Anshul Rana
March 18, 2026
11 min read

If you have been paying attention to the search industry over the past two years, you have probably seen three acronyms come up repeatedly: SEO, AEO, and GEO. Each represents a different approach to getting your brand discovered online, but they are not competing strategies. They are layers of the same system.

This article breaks down exactly what each one means, where they overlap, and how to build a unified strategy that covers all three.

SEO: The Foundation

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) is the practice of optimizing your website to rank higher in traditional search engine results, primarily Google. This has been the core of digital marketing for over two decades, and it is not going away.

SEO focuses on technical performance (site speed, crawlability, Core Web Vitals), on-page optimization (title tags, headers, content quality, internal linking), off-page authority (backlinks, brand mentions, domain authority), and local visibility (Google Business Profile, local citations, reviews).

SEO remains essential because Google still processes billions of searches daily. Organic search traffic is still the highest-intent traffic most businesses receive. Even AI search platforms pull from well-optimized, high-ranking pages. My core SEO services cover all of these fundamentals.

AEO: The Answering Layer

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring your content so that AI-powered answer engines, including ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Bing Copilot, and Google's AI Overviews, can cite and recommend your brand.

Where SEO asks "How do I rank for this keyword?", AEO asks "How do I become the answer to this question?" The distinction matters because AEO requires a different set of optimizations beyond traditional ranking factors.

AEO focuses on entity optimization (making your brand a recognized entity in AI knowledge graphs), structured data implementation (comprehensive schema markup), content formatting for AI consumption (direct answers, FAQ structures, comparison tables), and third-party corroboration (mentions across authoritative external sources).

GEO: The AI-Native Layer

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the broadest of the three. It encompasses the full strategy of optimizing your brand's digital footprint so that generative AI platforms feature your business in their outputs.

GEO includes everything in AEO, plus a focus on brand presence in AI training data (ensuring your brand appears frequently and positively in the data LLMs are trained on), real-time retrieval optimization (making sure models with web access can find and parse your content), multi-platform signal building (Quora, Reddit, industry forums, podcasts, and more), and llms.txt and other LLM-specific optimizations.

Think of GEO as the strategic umbrella. AEO is one part of it, focused on answer engines specifically. GEO covers the entire AI ecosystem.

How They Work Together

Here is the simplest way to think about the relationship:

SEO gets you ranked on Google. AEO gets you cited in AI answers. GEO makes sure your brand shows up across every AI platform and interaction.

They are not separate strategies. They are layers that build on each other. A strong SEO foundation (fast site, quality content, solid backlinks) makes your AEO efforts more effective. Strong AEO signals (entity clarity, structured data, external mentions) feed into your GEO visibility. And GEO, in turn, drives brand awareness that strengthens your traditional SEO.

Which One Do You Need?

The honest answer: you need all three. But the priority depends on where your business is right now.

If you are starting from scratch, focus on SEO first. Build a technically sound website, create quality content targeting high-intent keywords, and establish your basic online presence. Without this foundation, AEO and GEO efforts will not have much to build on.

If you already have strong SEO, layer in AEO. Implement comprehensive schema markup, structure your content for AI consumption, and start building entity signals across the web. Test your AI visibility regularly by asking LLMs about your industry.

If you are in a competitive niche where AI search is already driving purchase decisions, invest in the full GEO strategy. This means aggressive third-party signal building, llms.txt implementation, ongoing AI visibility monitoring, and continuous content optimization for both traditional and AI search.
